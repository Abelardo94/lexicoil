# Notes for the generator (read before the first prompt)

You are writing original Cambridge B1 Preliminary material that will be published to real students.
The prompt file is the spec. These notes add the quality bar a strict Cambridge reviewer applies;
they come from defects found in earlier rounds.

1. **Exactly one defensible answer per item.** For every MCQ, cloze, gapped-text and matching item,
   ask: could a strong B1 candidate argue for a second option? If yes, rewrite the distractor or the text.
   - Part 5 (MC cloze): distractors must be clearly wrong in THIS context (collocation, grammar or
     meaning), not merely less natural. Avoid near-synonyms (stay/sleep, experience/feel, choice/purchase).
   - Part 6 (open cloze): only ONE word may fit each gap. Gap closed-class words inside fixed
     constructions (so ... THAT, responsible FOR, had EVER, the ONE who, AS soon as). Never gap
     although/though, which/that, because/since, every/any, before/when or anything with a second valid word.
   - Part 4 (gapped text): each removed sentence must fit only its own gap — use reference words
     (this, they, however, then) that tie it to the sentences before and after. The 3 extra sentences
     must be on-topic but clearly impossible in every gap.
   - Part 2 (matching): each person has 2-3 requirements; exactly one text meets all of them, and at
     least one other text meets some of them (that is the distractor).
2. **Explanations must match the key and the text.** Quote or paraphrase the exact words in the text
   that give the answer, and say why the main distractor is wrong. Never refer to options by a letter
   that could change (write the option's words, not "option b").
3. **Listening Part 2 tests gist, attitude, opinion, purpose or agreement**, not isolated facts.
   Listening Part 4 tests opinions and attitudes of the interviewee.
4. Listening transcripts: natural spoken British English, speakers named consistently
   (e.g. "Woman:", "Man:", or first names). No German labels ("Aufnahme") anywhere.
5. **British spelling** (colour, centre, organise, programme). No American forms.
6. Do not repeat the passage title as the first line of the passage text.
7. Word counts in the prompt are hard limits. Count them.
8. Speaking Part 2: the task text shown to the candidate is an instruction such as
   "Look at the photograph. It shows ... Tell us what you can see." Do NOT write "(Imagine a colour photo...)".
9. Answer letters: spread them, as the prompt says.

Output: the JSON object only, exactly as the prompt specifies.
